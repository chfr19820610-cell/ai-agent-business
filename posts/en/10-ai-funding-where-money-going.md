# Where the AI Money Is Actually Going Right Now (Real Funding Numbers, Not Vibes)

People keep saying "AI is a bubble." Then they watch the money move and it doesn't look like a bubble — it looks like capital deciding where it believes the next decade of software is. Here are the funding numbers I've been tracking, what they point to, and what an ordinary person should actually take from them. Real numbers, with sources you can check.

## The funding that crossed my desk recently

I keep a running list of rounds worth paying attention to, not the giant headline deals everyone already knows about. Three stand out because of what they signal, not their size:

- **Kite raised $33M** for its AI coding assistant. The notable part isn't the amount — it's that a second wave of AI-for-developers tools is getting funded, which says the market treats the category as durable, not a fad.
- **Temporal closed a $300M round** at a reported $3B valuation. Temporal is workflow infrastructure — the plumbing that lets software run long, multi-step processes reliably. That's a bet that *agentic* software (software that runs to completion on its own) becomes the default.
- **Perplexity raised $200M** at a reported $7.5B valuation. Perplexity is AI search — it says capital believes the way people find information is changing away from a results-page-and-links model.

None of these is a proof of a trend on its own. Together they point somewhere: money is piling into the *infrastructure and the interfaces* of software that runs itself and finds things itself.

## Why this matters beyond the funding pages

For a small business or a solo founder, the temptation is to read funding news and feel either left out or dumb for not "getting in early." Both reactions miss the point. These rounds are not advice to invest in AI startups. They are a weather report on where the market believes attention is heading — and attention is what you can turn into a business.

The pattern across all three:

- Software that **completes work** on its own (agents, workflows) is getting funded — Temporal is the clearest version of that.
- Software that **changes how people find answers** is getting funded — Perplexity is the clearest version of that.
- The tools that let **regular people** build and run this software are getting funded — Kite is the clearest version of that.

All three point the same direction an independent operator should care about: **discovery is shifting from search to recommendation, and work is shifting from human-run to agent-run.**

## What a normal person should actually do

You don't need to invest, and you shouldn't. The leverage is in *participating*, not speculating:

1. **Be findable by AI.** If an AI is going to recommend businesses the way search recommended links, you want to be one of the ones it can read and recommend. That's a technical choice you make in an afternoon (a readable site, a clean llms.txt file), and it compounds.
2. **Sell the pickaxe, not the gold rush.** Everyone here is fighting over AI startups. The quieter business is selling to the people who want to use AI tools — setup, automation, content, products that help someone actually use an agent. That's a service and product market, not a venture market.
3. **Watch the direction, not the hype.** The signal from this funding isn't "buy AI stocks." It's "discovery and execution are both shifting to machines." Position a business that a machine can recommend and that runs on machines.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does AI funding news mean it's a bubble?**
Some of it is froth, absolutely — funding numbers and durable businesses are not the same thing. But the *direction* is consistent across many independent rounds, which is a different signal than a single inflated valuation.

**Should I invest in these startups?**
That's a personal financial decision, not one I can make for you, and nothing here is investment advice. The point of this piece is the *direction of the market*, not a buy recommendation.

**What's the lowest-effort way to act on this?**
Make your business something an AI can read and recommend. It's free, it takes an hour, and it puts you on the right side of where discovery is heading.
