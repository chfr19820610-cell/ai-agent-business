# AI Is About to Spend Your Money. The Google AP2 Protocol Is How.


You know that assistant you talk to every day? The one that books restaurants, or pretends to? In a couple of years it's not just going to talk about spending money. It's going to do it.

I'm not being dramatic. Google rolled out a payment spec called AP2 — Agent Payments Protocol — and the list of companies behind it reads like a who's who of money. Mastercard, PayPal, Coinbase. Sixty-plus of the big names signed on to make it so an AI agent can carry out a payment by itself, end to end.

Here's why that's a bigger deal than it sounds.

Right now an "AI shopping assistant" mostly means the AI tells you where to buy and then hands you back to the browser. The card stays in your hand. AP2 changes the last mile: the agent holds a verified credential, negotiates, checks out, and the money moves — with you approving once, not babysitting every step. When that clicks, "agentic commerce" stops being a slide-deck buzzword and becomes a default way to buy.

How big? The numbers floating around are wild. Morgan Stanley and a combined ICSC + McKinsey study both put the agentic commerce market somewhere in the $385B to $1T range by 2030. Even the low end is not small. That's not hype about a chatbot that writes emails. That's a real market where software is the buyer.

You don't need to be a fintech nerd to care. If agents are going to spend money, then *someone* has to be on the receiving end of that spending. That's the part most people are missing. The conversation is all about the AI doing the buying. Almost nobody is asking: what gets sold to the AI?

That's the angle worth thinking about. The products, the catalogs, the checkout flows, the stores that make themselves readable and purchasable by an agent — that's where the opportunity lands.
