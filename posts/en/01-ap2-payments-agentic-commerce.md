# AI Agents Will Start Spending Your Money. The Google AP2 Protocol Is How, and the Real Question Is Who Gets Paid.

The assistant that books your restaurant already exists. In a couple of years it won't stop at booking. It'll pay too.

That's the plan behind AP2, the Agent Payments Protocol that Google announced in 2025 with more than 60 companies behind it, including Mastercard, PayPal, Coinbase, and Revolut. The idea is that an AI agent can complete a purchase on its own: hold a verified credential, negotiate, check out, and move the money, with you approving once instead of watching every step.

The technology matters less than where the money lands.

Today an "AI shopping assistant" mostly tells you where to buy and hands you back to the browser. AP2 is the last mile. Once an agent can actually finish a purchase, agentic commerce stops being a slide-deck phrase and becomes a normal way to buy.

Size is harder to pin down than the headlines suggest. Morgan Stanley estimated $190 to $385 billion of U.S. e-commerce spending flowing through agentic shoppers by 2030 (Morgan Stanley research, 2025). McKinsey separately put the U.S. B2C retail upside closer to $1 trillion. The two firms are counting different things, so treat any single number as a range. Even the low end is a real market.

The part of this that gets little attention: if agents spend money, someone has to be on the receiving end. Almost all the coverage is about the AI doing the buying. The quieter question is what gets sold to the AI, and that means products, catalogs, checkout flows, and stores that make themselves readable and purchasable by an agent. That side is where the opportunity sits, and right now it isn't crowded.
